12 Bathroom Color Ideas (With Wall Art to Match)

By Clay & Line

The fastest way to change how a bathroom feels isn't a renovation. It's color — on the walls, in the towels, and in the art you hang. Pick one palette, repeat it three times around the room, and even a small bathroom starts to look planned.

Below are 12 calm color ideas we keep coming back to, each with a set of three prints that carries the palette onto the wall. Every print comes in eight sizes, from 8×10 to 24×36, so the same idea works above a toilet, a vanity or a bathtub.

How to pick a bathroom color scheme

  • Start with what stays. Tiles, the tub and the vanity rarely change, so let them set the base color.
  • Choose one accent color and repeat it at least three times: art, a towel, a soap bottle or a plant.
  • Keep the third color quiet. Cream, sand or soft white gives the eye somewhere to rest.
  • Match the art to the accent, not the walls. A sage print on a white wall reads as "sage bathroom"; a white print on a sage wall disappears.

1. Terracotta and cream

Warm, earthy and easy to live with. Terracotta works with almost any wood tone and makes white tiles feel softer. On a peach or cream wall it looks sun-warmed; add a touch of charcoal so it doesn't feel too sweet.

2. Sage green and white

Sage is the color of calm bathrooms for a reason: it feels fresh without being cold. Keep the room white, bring the green in through the art, and add one real plant.

3. Blush pink and rust

A soft pink bathroom can look grown-up when you add a darker note. Rust and burnt orange keep blush from feeling like a nursery — and black fixtures sharpen the whole thing.

4. Navy and mustard

A coastal palette that isn't all stripes and anchors. Navy grounds the room; mustard adds a little sun. It works on gray walls as well as on white tile, and it loves dark bronze or brass fixtures.

5. Mocha and sand

For a spa-like, neutral bathroom. Layer warm browns, beige and cream, and let texture do the work: a waffle towel, a stone tray, a wooden stool.

6. Olive and mustard

A retro palette that feels warm rather than loud. Olive green and mustard love wood vanities, brown towels and vintage brass.

7. Black and white

Timeless, graphic and good in small spaces. Keep it from feeling stark with one warm element — a wooden bath tray, a plant or a cream towel.

8. Dusty blue and peach

Soft blue calms a room; a little peach warms it back up. A gentle palette that also sits well on pale green or gray walls.

9. Lavender and deep purple

Lavender is quietly back. You don't need purple walls: in a white bathroom, lavender art with a deeper plum note is enough, and it pairs surprisingly well with a navy vanity.

10. Forest green and gold

The moody option. Deep green walls or tiles with gold accents feel rich in a powder room or guest bath, especially with warm light and fresh flowers.

11. Coral and teal

For a bathroom that makes you smile. Coral and teal sit opposite each other on the color wheel, so a little goes a long way — one print is enough on a tiled wall.

12. Charcoal and clay

Earthy and a little architectural. Charcoal adds depth; clay keeps it warm. Lovely on blue-gray walls, with white paneling and chrome or matte black taps.

Which size to choose

  • Above a toilet: one 12×16 or 16×20 print, or three 8×10 or 11×14 prints hung 2–3 inches apart.
  • Above a vanity: match the width of the mirror or slightly less — three 11×14 or 12×16 prints work for most single vanities.
  • Above a bathtub: go bigger — three 16×20 or 18×24 prints.
  • Hang the center of the group at eye level, roughly 57–60 inches from the floor, or about 8–10 inches above the toilet tank or faucet.

Quick FAQ

Can I put prints in a bathroom? Yes — use a frame with glass or acrylic and avoid hanging art where water splashes directly. A bathroom with an extractor fan or window is ideal.

Should bathroom art match the towels? It doesn't have to match exactly; picking up one color from the art in a towel or mat is enough to tie the room together.

Do I need all three prints? No. Every print is also sold on its own, and two prints from the same set work well above a toilet. The set of three is the better deal if you have the wall for it.
