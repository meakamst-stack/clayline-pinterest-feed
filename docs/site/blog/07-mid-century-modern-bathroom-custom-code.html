Mid Century Modern Bathroom Ideas (and the Wall Art That Finishes Them)

By Clay & Line

Mid century modern is the rare style that suits a bathroom as well as a living room. It is clean but not cold, colorful but not busy, and most of it comes down to a few simple choices: warm wood, honest shapes, one confident color and art with a bit of a wink.

You don't need to rip out tile to get there. These are the ideas that do the most work, in order of effort.

What makes a bathroom mid century modern

  • Warm wood. Walnut and teak tones, on a vanity, a stool, a shelf or just a picture frame.
  • Simple geometry. Square or stacked tile, round mirrors, arches, globe lights.
  • One bold color against a calm base: mustard, olive, teal, coral or pink.
  • Graphic art. Sunbursts, boomerangs, arcs and other playful abstract shapes.
  • Little clutter. Slim legs, clear surfaces, a single plant.

1. Start with a walnut frame

If you change one thing, make it this. A walnut-toned frame against white tile brings in the wood that defines the style, without touching the vanity. Pair it with a sunburst — the atomic starburst is the most recognizable mid century motif there is.

2. Keep (or fake) the colored tile

Pink bathrooms were everywhere in 1950s homes, and the ones that survived are now the envy of anyone who loves the era. If you have original pink, mint or blue tile, keep it and style around it. If you don't, a wall of simple square tile in one warm color gives the same effect.

Colored tile wants art that agrees with it, not art that fights it. Pick prints that repeat the tile color and add one contrast: coral with a deep teal is a classic pairing.

The art: the Coral Pop set of 3.

3. Use boomerangs, arcs and other period shapes

Mid century graphics are friendly: boomerangs, kidney shapes, dots and loose arcs, usually two or three flat colors on a pale ground. One such print is enough for a small wall. A thin black frame keeps it crisp on a tiled or colored wall.

4. Add a sunburst to a plain bathroom

No retro tile, no wood vanity? A neutral, builder-grade bathroom still takes the style well. Gray walls, a dark vanity and one navy and mustard sunburst next to the mirror is a five-minute version of the look. Repeat the navy once more in a towel or the shower curtain and it reads as intentional.

5. Go olive and mustard

The other great mid century palette is the earthy one: olive green, mustard and warm brown. It is the natural match for a wood vanity and brass hardware, and it makes a beige bathroom look like a choice rather than a default.

The art: the Olive Retro set of 3.

6. Finish with the small things

  • A round or pill-shaped mirror instead of a rectangular one.
  • A globe or cone wall light in brass or matte black.
  • A wooden stool or bath tray.
  • One leafy plant: a palm, a rubber plant or a snake plant.
  • Towels in a single solid color taken from the art.

Which size to hang

  • Next to a mirror or on a narrow wall: one 11×14 or 12×16 print.
  • Above a toilet: one 12×16 or 16×20 print, or three 8×10 prints in a row.
  • Above a bathtub: three 16×20 or 18×24 prints, 2–3 inches apart.

Quick FAQ

What colors are mid century modern? Warm wood tones with mustard, olive, teal, burnt orange, coral and soft pink, set against white, cream or gray.

Can a small bathroom be mid century modern? Yes. The style relies on clean lines and a few strong accents, which suits small rooms. One print, a wood detail and a round mirror are enough.

What frame suits mid century art? Walnut or another warm wood is the most typical. Thin black works well on colored tile, white on dark walls.
