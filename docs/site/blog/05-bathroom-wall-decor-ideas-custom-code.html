Bathroom Wall Decor Ideas: What to Hang and Where (With Sizes)

By Clay & Line

Most bathrooms have three or four spots where wall art works, and a few where it really doesn't. The trick is matching the size and number of prints to the wall you've got: one print for a narrow strip, a pair for the space above the toilet, a set of three above a long tub.

Here's what to hang in each spot, with the sizes and heights that look right. Every photo is a real bathroom with our prints on the wall.

1. Above the tub: a set of three

The wall above a bathtub is usually the biggest empty wall in the room, and the one you look at while you're in the bath. A set of three prints in a row fills it without crowding. Keep the whole row narrower than the tub (roughly two thirds of its length), leave 2–3 inches between frames and hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above the tub rim or tile line.

2. Above the toilet: one print

For a single print over the toilet, go bigger than you think: 16×20 or 18×24 for most bathrooms. Center it on the toilet, not on the wall, and hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above the tank or flush plate. In a room with colored tile, pick a print that repeats the tile color.

The art: the Blush Pink Geometric print from the Blush Geometric set of 3.

3. Above the toilet: a pair

Two prints side by side fill the width above a toilet better than one small frame. Use two designs from the same set so the colors match, the same frame for both, and 2–3 inches between them. 11×14 or 12×16 is the usual size; in a bathroom with high ceilings go up to 18×24.

The art: two prints from the Lavender Night set of 3, in oak frames.

4. Above the toilet: a stack of three

In a narrow toilet room there's often no width for a pair. Stack three small prints (8×10 or 11×14) in a column instead; it draws the eye up and makes the room feel taller. See examples in our small bathroom decor ideas.

5. Between the shower and the mirror

The strip of wall between a shower curtain and a vanity mirror is easy to forget, and it's the perfect spot for one tall print. Keep it out of direct splash range, use a frame with glass or acrylic, and center it at eye level (57–60 inches from the floor).

The art: the Navy and Mustard Rainbow print in a black frame.

6. On the side wall

In a half bath the side wall next to the toilet is often the only big empty wall. One large print there (18×24 or 20×30) makes the room feel finished from the doorway. Hang it at eye level from where you'd stand, not from the toilet.

The art: the Navy Sunburst print from the Coastal Sun set of 3.

7. Around a corner

If a wall is too short for three frames, wrap the set around the corner: two on the long wall, one on the short one. Keep the tops of all frames on one line so it still reads as a group.

The art: the Charcoal Clay set of 3.

8. Above the towel bar

A towel bar on an otherwise blank wall looks lonely. Hang one print centered above it, 10–12 inches higher, so towels never touch the frame.

9. On a ledge or shelf

No drilling? Lean a framed print on a ledge, the back of the vanity or a shelf above the toilet. Layer a smaller frame or a plant in front for a relaxed look.

10. Over the vanity: skip it

The wall above the sink belongs to the mirror. Instead of squeezing art next to it, upgrade the mirror (round, arched, wood or brass) and use the wall beside it.

Bathroom wall art sizes at a glance

  • Above a toilet (single): 16×20 or 18×24.
  • Above a toilet (pair): two 11×14 or 12×16.
  • Narrow toilet room: three 8×10 stacked, or one 16×20.
  • Above a tub: three 12×16 or 16×20 in a row, or two 18×24.
  • Beside a mirror or shower: one 11×14 to 18×24, depending on the wall.
  • Side wall of a half bath: one 18×24 or 20×30.

How high to hang art in a bathroom

  • On an open wall: center of the art at 57–60 inches from the floor.
  • Above a toilet, tub or towel bar: bottom of the frame 8–12 inches above it.
  • Groups: treat the whole group as one piece; same gaps (2–3 inches) everywhere.

Paper prints are fine in a ventilated bathroom: use a frame with glass or acrylic, keep them out of direct splash, and run the fan after showers. Quick FAQ

What can I hang on my bathroom walls? Framed prints, a mirror, open shelves, a towel ladder or hooks. Prints in glass-fronted frames are the easiest way to add color and personality.

What size art should go above a toilet? One 16×20 or 18×24 print, or two 11×14 or 12×16 prints side by side. The art should be at least half as wide as the toilet tank or wider.

How high should art be above a toilet? Hang the bottom of the frame 8–10 inches above the tank or flush plate, centered on the toilet.

Is it OK to hang a gallery wall in a bathroom? Yes. A set of three in matching frames is easiest; keep equal gaps and treat the group as one piece when you center it.
