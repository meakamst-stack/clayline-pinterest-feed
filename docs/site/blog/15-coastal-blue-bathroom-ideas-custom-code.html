Coastal Bathroom Decor Ideas: 10 Calm Ways to Use Blue

By Clay & Line

A coastal bathroom doesn't need anchors, seashells or a sign that says "beach". The look that lasts is simpler: light blue or blue-gray walls, white tile, a bit of navy, natural wood and textures that feel washed by the sea.

These coastal bathroom decor ideas show how to use blue in a calm, grown-up way, from pale paneling to a navy vanity, with real bathrooms and our prints on the wall.

1. Let one navy piece lead

A navy vanity, a navy door or a navy shower curtain gives a white bathroom instant coastal character. Pick up the navy once more at eye level, in the art or the towels, and add one warm accent — mustard, sand or brass — so it doesn't feel cold.

2. Use pale blue paint or paneling

Pale blue on the walls is the classic coastal base. Vertical paneling or beadboard in a soft blue adds texture and makes a small room look taller; white tile on the lower half keeps it fresh.

The art: the Ocean Waves Sunset print in a light oak frame.

3. Try blue-gray for a softer coast

Blue-gray reads like a cloudy day by the sea: calm, a little moody and very easy to live with. It works especially well in a half bath, with white fixtures and one large print in navy and mustard.

The art: the Navy and Mustard Rainbow print in a black frame.

4. Add stripes

Stripes are the most coastal pattern there is. A striped shower curtain, striped towels or a striped rug brings in navy without painting anything. Repeat one of the stripe colors in the art.

The art: the Ocean Waves Sunset print in a black frame.

5. Pair white wainscoting with natural wood

White wainscoting, a wood floor and a wood-framed mirror feel like a beach cottage. Add dried grasses or a seagrass basket and a navy and mustard print, and you're done.

The art: the Navy Sunburst print in an oak frame.

6. Go soft with dusty blue and sage

Not every coastal bathroom has to be navy. Dusty blue, sea glass green and sage-gray feel quieter and work well with a bit of peach or sand for warmth.

The art: the Dusty Blue Line Arch print from the Dusty Blue set of 3.

7. Choose sun and sea in the art, not souvenirs

Simple shapes — a low sun, waves, a horizon line — say coastal without looking like a gift shop. Stick to two or three colors: navy, mustard and sand, or dusty blue and peach.

8. Bring in natural textures

Seagrass baskets, a rope or wood mirror, a teak bath mat and linen towels give a coastal bathroom its relaxed feel. Weathered wood and rattan work better than anything shiny.

9. Keep towels and textiles simple

White towels with a navy stripe, or towels in one soft blue, look crisp and coastal. Skip the novelty prints.

10. Keep it light and bright

White tile, clear glass, light grout and plenty of light keep blue from closing a room in. Brushed nickel and brass both suit a coastal bathroom; matte black adds a modern edge.

Blue bathroom colors that work

  • Pale sky blue: fresh and classic; best with white and natural wood.
  • Blue-gray: soft and moody; great in half baths.
  • Navy: for one strong piece — a vanity, a door or the art.
  • Dusty blue and sage: quiet and calm, with peach or sand as accents.
  • Accents: mustard, sand, warm wood and brass keep blue from feeling cold.

Quick FAQ

How do I make my bathroom look coastal without being tacky? Skip anchors and shells. Use light blue or blue-gray walls, white tile, natural wood and rattan, striped or plain textiles, and simple sun-and-sea wall art in two or three colors.

What colors go with a blue bathroom? White and cream, sand and natural wood, mustard or brass for warmth, and navy or dusty blue as a second shade of blue.

What wall art suits a coastal bathroom? Minimal prints with waves, suns and horizons in navy, mustard, sand or dusty blue, framed in oak or black.

Is navy too dark for a small bathroom? Not if you use it on one piece — a vanity, a door or the art — and keep the walls and tile light.
