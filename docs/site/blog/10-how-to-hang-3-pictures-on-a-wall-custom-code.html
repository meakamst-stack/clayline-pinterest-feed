How to Hang 3 Pictures on a Wall (Spacing, Height and Size)

By Clay & Line

A set of three prints is the easiest way to make a bathroom wall look finished. It's also easy to get slightly wrong: hung too high, spaced too far apart or too small for the wall.

Here's how to hang 3 pictures on a wall so they look like one piece, with the spacing, height and sizes that work above a toilet, above a tub and on a narrow wall. Every photo is a real bathroom with our prints on the wall.

The short answer: leave 2–3 inches between the frames. On an empty wall, center the group 57–60 inches from the floor. Above a toilet, tub or vanity, hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above it instead. Keep the row about two-thirds as wide as the tub or vanity below it, and center it on that fixture, not on the wall.

The three numbers that matter

Get these three right and the rest is easy.

  • Spacing: 2–3 inches between frames. Closer than 2 inches looks crowded; past 4 inches the three stop reading as a set. With large frames (18×24 and up) you can go to 3–4 inches.
  • Height: 57–60 inches to the center. On an open wall, the center of the whole group sits at eye level, the height galleries use. Over a toilet, tub or vanity, the fixture sets the height: hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above it.
  • Width: about two-thirds of what's below. Art over a tub or vanity looks best at roughly two-thirds of its width (the 2/3 rule). A toilet is narrow, so a row above it can be wider than the toilet itself; just leave a few inches of wall on each side.

How to hang a set of 3 prints, step by step

You need a tape measure, a pencil, a level, painter's tape, some kraft or wrapping paper and three nails or picture hooks.

  1. Find your center line. Measure the toilet, tub or vanity and mark its center on the wall. Center the set on the fixture, not on the wall: that's what the eye compares it to.
  2. Make paper templates. Trace each frame on paper and cut it out. Mark on each template where the nail goes: pull the wire taut or measure down to the sawtooth hanger.
  3. Tape them up. Put the templates on the wall with painter's tape and equal gaps. Step back, look from the doorway and adjust until the height and spacing feel right.
  4. Hang the middle frame first. Nail through the mark on the middle template, tear the paper off, hang the frame and level it.
  5. Measure the side hooks. When the hooks sit in the middle of each frame, the next hook is one frame width plus one gap away. Frames 14 inches wide with 2½-inch gaps: hooks 16½ inches apart, all at the same height.
  6. Hang the sides and check. Hang the outer frames, lay a level across the tops and fine-tune. Small bumpers or a bit of poster putty on the bottom corners keep them straight.

In a steamy bathroom, adhesive strips can let go; a nail or picture hook is more reliable. Use frames with glass or acrylic and keep prints out of direct shower spray.

Above the toilet: a row of three

Above a toilet, a row of three fills the width better than one small frame. Hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above the tank lid (or above the flush plate on a wall-hung toilet), and center the row on the toilet. 11×14 or 12×16 suits most bathrooms; in a narrow toilet alcove, use 8×10 or stack the prints.

Go bigger than you think

The most common mistake is frames that are too small for the wall. Three small prints on a big wall look like a strip; the same wall with bigger frames looks planned. If the wall between the tile line and the ceiling still looks empty, go up a size.

On a narrow wall: stack them

No room for a row? Stack the three prints in a column. It works in a narrow toilet room or on the strip of wall beside a mirror, and it makes a low ceiling feel taller. Keep 2 inches between frames, center the column on the toilet and hang the bottom frame 8–10 inches above the tank. 8×10 or 11×14 is the right size.

Above the tub

The wall above a bathtub is usually the biggest empty wall in the bathroom. Keep the row about two-thirds of the tub length: above a standard 60-inch tub that's roughly 40 inches, so three 11×14 or 12×16 prints. Hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above the rim or the tile line. If the tub doubles as a shower, keep the frames out of the spray and choose acrylic over glass.

Behind a freestanding tub

A freestanding tub sits away from the wall, so the art can be bigger: 16×20 or 18×24. Center the row on the tub rather than on the wall, and hang it a little higher than over a built-in tub so it clears the faucet and anything on a bath tray.

Around a corner

If the wall above the tub is short, hang two prints on that wall and the third on the next wall, at exactly the same height. Keep the distance to the corner equal on both sides and the set still reads as one.

Next to the vanity

Over a vanity, the mirror usually takes the space. Hang the set on the side wall instead: a row above a towel bar, or a column beside the mirror. Line the top of the set up with the top of the mirror so the two look planned together.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Hanging too high. If you have to look up at the art, lower it.
  • Gaps that are too wide. Past 4 inches, three frames look like three separate pictures.
  • Centering on the wall. Center on the toilet, tub or vanity instead.
  • Frames that are too small. When in doubt, go up a size.
  • Mixed frames. Use the same frame for all three.
  • Three unrelated prints. Prints that share one palette and one style look calm; a random mix looks busy in a small room.

Quick FAQ

How far apart should 3 pictures be hung? 2–3 inches between frames. With large frames (18×24 and up) you can go to 3–4 inches.

How high should I hang 3 pictures? On an empty wall, center the group 57–60 inches from the floor. Above a toilet, tub or vanity, hang the bottom edge 8–10 inches above it.

What is the 2/3 rule for hanging pictures? Art above furniture or a fixture should be about two-thirds of its width. Above a 60-inch tub, that's a row of about 40 inches.

Should I hang 3 pictures in a row or vertically? A row for wider walls, like above a tub or a toilet with space on both sides; a vertical stack for narrow walls and small toilet rooms.

What size pictures go above a toilet? Three 11×14 or 12×16 prints in a row for most bathrooms; 8×10 for a narrow alcove.

Can I hang 3 pictures of different sizes? Yes. Put the largest in the middle and line up the centers (or the bottom edges) of all three. Same-size frames are easier, and calmer in a small bathroom.
