Small Bathroom Decor Ideas: 12 Simple Ways to Make It Feel Bigger

By Clay & Line

A small bathroom doesn't need less decor — it needs fewer, better choices. One good print instead of five little ones. One color that repeats instead of six that compete. Storage that goes up instead of out.

These small bathroom decor ideas are simple, most of them cost little, and every photo below is a real small bathroom or toilet room with our prints on the wall, so you can see what each idea actually looks like.

1. Hang one large print instead of several small ones

It sounds backwards, but a big print makes a small room feel bigger. A cluster of tiny frames chops the wall into pieces; one generous print gives the eye a single place to land. In a narrow toilet room, an 18×24 print centered over the toilet is usually right, and on a tall wall a 24×36 still won't feel too much.

2. Stack small frames vertically

If you love small prints, stack them. Three 8×10 or 11×14 frames hung in a column above the toilet draw the eye up, which makes a low or narrow room feel taller. Keep the gaps the same (2 inches is plenty) and use identical frames so the stack reads as one piece.

The art: the Mono Minimal set of 3 in black frames with white mats.

3. Keep the walls light and let the art bring the color

White, warm white or the palest gray bounces light around a room with no window. That doesn't mean the room has to feel bland: one print with a soft color — sage, blush, dusty blue — gives a white toilet room personality without closing it in. Pick a color you can repeat in a hand towel or soap bottle.

The art: the Minimalist Plant Stem print in sage and blush, in a thin black frame.

4. Add wainscoting or tile on the lower half

Splitting the wall in two — paneling, beadboard or tile below, paint above — gives a small bathroom structure and protects the wall where it gets splashed. Keep the top half light and hang the art above the line, with the bottom of the frame 6–10 inches above the paneling.

The art: the Ocean Waves Sunset print in a light oak frame.

5. Or go moody on purpose

A tiny bathroom will never feel huge, so some people stop trying and make it cozy instead. Deep or muted green, navy or charcoal walls blur the corners and make a small room feel intentional and a little dramatic. The trick is contrast: a light sink, a light print and good lighting so it doesn't turn into a cave.

The art: the Green Mid Century Circle print in a black frame.

6. Choose a warm color instead of stark white

If your bathroom has no natural light, bright white can look gray and flat. A warm yellow, peach or cream feels sunnier in artificial light and flatters skin in the mirror. Pick art in the same warm family — terracotta, rust, mustard — so the room feels finished rather than busy.

The art: the Boho Rainbow Sun print in terracotta and blush, in a thin black frame.

7. Repeat one color from the tile

Colored tile you can't change is a gift, not a problem: pull one shade out of it and repeat it in the art. Peach tile with a rust print, green tile with a sage print, blue tile with a navy print. The room instantly looks planned.

The art: the Rust Star Geometric print in a black frame.

8. Use the wall beside the mirror

In a small bathroom the space above the toilet isn't the only option. The strip of wall next to the mirror or between the vanity and the tub is often the best spot for a single print, and it's seen every time someone washes their hands. Hang it with the center at eye level, around 57–60 inches from the floor.

The art: the Blue Ripple Star print in a black frame.

9. Hang a pair above the toilet

Two prints side by side fill the width above a toilet better than one small frame, and they look more deliberate. Use the same frame and size for both, leave 2–3 inches between them, and keep the pair a little narrower than the tank.

The art: two prints from the Olive Retro set of 3, in black frames.

10. Store vertically

Floor space is what makes a bathroom feel small, so keep it clear. A narrow shelf or cabinet above the toilet, hooks on the back of the door, a recessed niche in the shower and a slim rolling cart beside the vanity all take things off the floor and the counter.

11. Choose a wall-hung sink and a round mirror

A wall-mounted sink or a vanity on legs shows more floor, which makes the room read bigger. A round or oval mirror softens all the hard lines of tile and fixtures. You can see both in the first and fourth photos above.

12. Edit what's on display

In a small bathroom everything is in view, so be choosy. One plant that likes humidity (pothos, a fern or a snake plant), one nice soap, matching towels in one or two colors and a small tray to corral the rest. Everything else goes in a drawer or a basket.

Small bathroom decor on a budget

  • Paint first. A quart of paint changes a small bathroom more than anything else you can buy.
  • Swap the small things: towel ring, toilet roll holder and cabinet knobs in one matching finish (black, brass or chrome).
  • Use standard frame sizes. Our prints come in 8 sizes that fit ready-made frames, so you don't need custom framing.
  • Buy a set of 3 and split it. Hang two in the bathroom and the third in the hallway outside, so the rooms connect.
  • Add one plant instead of lots of small accessories.
  • Replace the shower curtain with a plain linen or waffle one in a color from the art.

What size art works in a small bathroom?

  • Above the toilet in a narrow toilet room: one 16×20 or 18×24 print, or three 8×10 prints stacked vertically.
  • Above a toilet with a vanity next to it: two 11×14 or 12×16 prints side by side.
  • Next to the mirror: one 11×14 or 12×16 print, center at 57–60 inches from the floor.
  • On a tall wall: a single 24×36 makes a bold statement without crowding.

Quick FAQ

How do I make a small bathroom look bigger? Keep the floor clear, use light walls or one bold dark color, choose a wall-hung sink and a round mirror, and hang one large print or a vertical stack instead of many small frames.

What is the best decor for a small bathroom? A few things that repeat one color: one print or a matching pair, towels and a soap bottle in a shade from the art, and one plant. Fewer, larger pieces look calmer than lots of small accessories.

Should you put wall art in a small bathroom? Yes. Art is one of the few things that adds personality without taking up floor space. Choose a frame with glass or acrylic and keep it out of direct splash range.

How can I decorate a small bathroom on a budget? Paint the walls, swap hardware to one finish, add one plant and hang art in standard-size frames. A set of 3 prints covers the bathroom and the hallway outside it.
